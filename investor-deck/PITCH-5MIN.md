# MockTrade Matrix — 5-Minute Pitch

**4 speakers · 17 slides · ~300 seconds**

| Speaker | Time | Slides | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — You | 0:00–1:15 | 1–3 | Hook, problem, insight |
| 2 | 1:15–2:20 | 4–7 | Product, interface, journey, trust |
| 3 | 2:20–3:30 | 8–12 | Behaviour, institution, market, model, GTM |
| 4 | 3:30–4:45 | 13–17 | Competition, traction, roadmap, ask, close |

> Pace: ~135 words/minute. Pause on every **/** mark. Bold words get emphasis. Hand the clicker before you start.

---

## SPEAKER 1 — YOU (0:00–1:15)

**Slide 1 — Cover. Stand still. No clicker yet.**

Good morning. I want to start with a question.

**/** What did losing money teach you that a textbook never did?

**/** *(pause — let it land)* For most of us, the answer is: everything. And it cost us something.

Now imagine that lesson arriving at **seventeen** — in a simulation, with a teacher and a guardian in the room — instead of at twenty-five, with a real salary.

**Slide 2 — Problem.**

Only **33%** of adults worldwide are financially literate. In India, **27%**. We teach teenagers about risk with slides — then hand them a brokerage app and hope.

**/** Point to the gap. That space between the classroom and the first real trade is where confidence, discipline and money are lost. **Nobody practises the gap.**

**Slide 3 — Insight.**

MockTrade Matrix is the rehearsal.

A guardian-verified markets lab where students trade **India and the US** with real market friction — spreads, fees, slippage, live prices — and **zero real money**. Five asset classes. Thirty-one instruments. Two markets. One behavioural report a parent can actually read.

Every investor pays for experience. **We make the first tuition cheap.**

**/** *(hand over)* Speaker two will show you what that feels like inside the product.

## SPEAKER 2 (1:15–2:20)

**Slide 4 — Product.**

Thank you. Let me show you what this actually is.

**Slide 5 — Interface.**

A student signs in — every visit, a real sign-in. No silent auto-login. Before they commit capital, they verify identity with a real document check, and a guardian approves through a shareable link. Then they start clean: **zero invested**, one lakh rupees or ten thousand dollars simulated. **No fake winning portfolio.**

On the trade screen it behaves like a terminal: live prices, an order ticket, real costs. Switch to the classroom and it behaves like a school: join code, modules, competitions. Same surface. The engine runs live.

**Slide 6 — Journey.**

But the difference is what we record. Not just profit and loss — **discipline. Patience. Risk control. Diversification. Learning.**

**Slide 7 — Trust.**

We only score what the student has actually done, and we show coverage. No activity, no score. Parents get a report they can read, not a dashboard they have to interpret. Identity is verified, consent is logged, and the document image is deleted after the check.

That trust layer is not a compliance page. **It is why a parent says yes.**

**/** *(hand over)*

## SPEAKER 3 (2:20–3:30)

**Slide 8 — Behaviour.**

For the institution, this becomes one console: classrooms, cohort analytics, competitions, support, and admin-only content publishing. Teachers see **real activity**, not estimates.

**Slide 9 — Institution.**

Access is granted per account by the administrator. Students join with a code. Pricing is simple: **₹2,499 a month per institution; ₹599 a month for a single educator.** Monthly, no lock-in. Sold to schools — the learner never pays to practise.

**Slide 10 — Market.**

Why now? Financial literacy is becoming a **required subject**. India's NEP 2020 moved it into the school conversation. Twenty-five US states now require a personal finance course. Curriculum creates budget. Budget creates a buyer. And the buyer needs evidence.

**Slide 11 — Model.**

The wedge is a teacher, not a procurement cycle. Land one classroom in thirty days, prove engagement, expand to the department, then the school, then the district.

**Slide 12 — GTM.**

Five hundred institutions at ₹2,499 is **₹1.5 crore ARR**. Five thousand educators is another **₹3.6 crore**. That is the shape of the business — not a forecast.

**/** *(hand over)*

## SPEAKER 4 (3:30–4:45)

**Slide 13 — Competition.**

Everyone in this market owns a slice. Paper trading has a terminal but no pedagogy. Curriculum vendors have pedagogy but no market. Consumer fintech has real money — which is exactly what we remove. **Nobody owns the loop: verify, practise, measure, report.**

**Slide 14 — Traction.**

And this is not slideware. Thirty-one live instruments. Fourteen product routes. A live market engine. Identity verification, guardian consent, institutional console, exports. **Thirty-six out of thirty-six institutional QA checks passed.** The product is running today.

**Slide 15 — Roadmap.**

Next: ten pilot classrooms. Then one hundred paying institutions and twenty-five thousand activated students in eighteen months.

**Slide 16 — Ask.**

We are raising **₹3.5 crore — about four hundred and twenty thousand dollars** — for an eighteen-month runway. Forty percent engineering. Thirty-five percent sales. Fifteen percent curriculum. Ten percent operations. The next hire is go-to-market, not another engineer.

The plan is deliberately boring: **sell the pilots, keep the classrooms, expand the seats.**

**Slide 17 — Close. Slow down. Look at the audience, not the screen.**

Every investor loses money once.

**/** We make sure it happens at seventeen — in a simulation, with a teacher and a guardian in the room — not at twenty-five, with a real salary.

**Learn to lose. Before it costs anything.**

**/** Thank you.

## If you are running long, cut these first

1. The “five asset classes” list on Slide 3.
2. The sentence about modules and competitions on Slide 5.
3. The support/admin-publishing detail on Slide 8.
4. The “plan is deliberately boring” line on Slide 16.

**Never cut:** the opening question, “zero real money”, “nobody owns the loop”, and the closing three lines.

## Delivery notes

- **Speaker 1:** do not rush the opening pause. Silence is the hook.
- **Speaker 2:** say “zero invested” slowly. It is the credibility line.
- **Speaker 3:** prices are the proof of a real business. Say them clearly, no apology.
- **Speaker 4:** the close is not a summary. It is a moral argument. Pause before the last line.
- Keep hands out of pockets. Plant your feet. One idea per sentence.
- If a slide fails, keep talking — the numbers are in the script.

## Q&A — 20-second answers

**“Why won't schools just build this?”**
They do not want to run a market engine, identity checks, guardian consent and live infrastructure. They want a classroom tool that works on Monday. We sell the outcome, not the software.

**“Is it safe for minors?”**
No real money. No deposits or withdrawals. Identity is verified only before simulated capital is committed, the document image is deleted, and a guardian approves through a logged consent link.

**“How do you acquire schools?”**
Teacher-led pilots. One classroom, thirty days, real student activity, a parent-ready report. Then the educator upgrades to an institution and expands by seats.

**“What stops a competitor copying you?”**
The trust layer and the data loop. Guardian consent, verified identity and behavioural history are hard to retrofit, and they compound with every classroom.

**“What is the biggest risk?”**
Sales cycle length. That is why the entry point is a teacher, not a principal — and why the pilot is free for thirty days.

**“Why four people?”**
One narrative, four proofs: the problem, the product, the business, the ask. No handoff loses energy.
